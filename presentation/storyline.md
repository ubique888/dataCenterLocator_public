# Storyline

**Opening tension:** Cheap land argues for remote sites. Inference, network access and potential heat reuse create a reason to examine urban industrial sites.

**Turn:** Density adds cost and permitting friction, but also brings users, carriers, workforce, grid assets and possible heat customers into reach. These are opportunities to test, not automatic benefits.

**Method:** The locator screens EPA land/grid records, enriches sites with retired power, manufacturing and wastewater context, and exposes infrastructure inheritance and industrial symbiosis as separate axes. A separate preview adds raw PeeringDB proximity indicators.

**Decision:** The high-high screen identifies Astoria and Salem. Astoria is the first regional diligence lead because the current evidence combines infrastructure inheritance with nearby potential resource users. The claim stops at screening.

**Future:** A deployment decision needs a parcel, utility-deliverable MW, measured user-to-building network performance, contracts, costs and environmental baselines. Gradual change in the metro customer base, scarcity of well-connected suburban land and long-lived infrastructure links can support phased planning across decades. Hardware and routing can adapt as demand changes. These are planning premises, not project-measured forecasts.

**Five-minute arc:** surprise (35 s), tradeoff (40 s), system diagram (45 s), product method (45 s), site comparison (40 s), recommendation (45 s), implementation vision (45 s). Target 295 s including brief transitions.
