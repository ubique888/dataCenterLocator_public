# Presentation brief

**Audience:** hackathon judges. **Runtime:** five minutes. **Format:** seven 16:9 slides, English on-slide copy, English speaker notes. **Decision:** advance Astoria/Queens as a due-diligence region, with Salem as comparison; do not claim a buildable parcel or verified network-first optimum.

**Source of truth:** `../docs/six_page_story.md`, `../README_DATA.md`, the project's validation outputs and two existing HTML demos. This deck makes no new technical or research claims.

**Core promise:** The locator moves the audience from “which region is promising?” to “which specific urban or brownfield record warrants parcel, utility and carrier diligence?” Its current implementation is a transparent geographic screen. The proposed metro inference and regional offload architecture is a future operating concept.

**Design:** restrained infrastructure aesthetic: deep navy, off-white, cool cyan for opportunity, warm amber for constraints. Large claim titles, spare labels, native diagrams and evidence from the existing project. No stock data-center photos or generic corporate art.

**Claim guardrails:** 190,976 EPA records; 8,479 screened sites; two independent opportunity axes; Astoria 78.46 inheritance and 93.21 symbiosis; Salem 61.19 and 79.99. For Astoria, the independent recount found 37 distinct FRS IDs within 5 km, representing 34 coordinate groups: food 3, beverage 4, paper 6, chemicals 15, primary metals 9. These are records in selected manufacturing categories and signal potential heat-reuse leads, not verified customers. Current operations, heat demand and temperature, pipe feasibility and willingness to partner are unknown. Three wastewater records are also within 5 km. Retired MW, line proximity, PeeringDB distance and CWNS design flow do not certify available power, low latency or reusable water. The source cohort requires at least 50 reported acres and may miss compact urban sites.

**Final-slide planning premises supplied by the user:** metro residents and firms tend to relocate gradually, so latency-sensitive demand may remain geographically anchored; suburban land near useful infrastructure is scarcer than rural acreage. These are long-horizon reasons to preserve a metro option, not measured demographic or land forecasts in the locator. Durable utility/carrier/heat links and replaceable compute add two related implications.
